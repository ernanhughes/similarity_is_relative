# Option C0 — Discovery and Confirmation Protocol for Propagated Refusal

## Status

**Prospective discovery protocol. No Option C mechanism has been selected, no C1 confirmatory contract exists, no final calibration or test rows have been selected, and no Option C scientific result has been observed.**

Option B established the bounded real-representation premise required to continue:

> A frozen representation can contain recoverable structural relations that its default cosine and Euclidean geometry materially underexpose.

Option C asks a different question:

> Can calibrated primitive evidence be propagated through a compound query so that the system refuses unsupported answers more effectively than simpler calibrated alternatives at matched coverage?

The project does not yet know the correct support object, propagation rule, query regime or non-degenerate coverage region. Freezing one mechanism now would combine invention and confirmation in the same test and risk repeating the central E01 mistake: obtaining a clean answer to a question that was not sufficiently understood.

This protocol therefore splits the authorised Option C budget into:

```text
C0  bounded mechanism discovery and discriminating-power review
C1  separately frozen confirmatory refusal experiment
```

C0 permits learning. C1 permits claim testing. They may not share a final holdout.

## Decision and budget

The remaining Option C budget is at most **15 working days** in total.

```text
C0 discovery phase: maximum 5 working days
C1 confirmation phase: remaining budget after C0, maximum total 15 days
```

Unused C0 time may remain available to C1. The total budget may not exceed 15 working days without a new explicit project decision.

C0 begins only after this protocol merges. C1 implementation is blocked until a later documentation-only C1 contract is reviewed and merged.

## Why C0 exists

Preregistration protects a confirmatory result from post-hoc goal changes. It should not force the project to pretend that an undeveloped mechanism is already understood.

Option B could be frozen directly because it asked one clean existence question with one already-defined executor. Option C contains an unresolved design problem:

- what constitutes primitive support;
- how support should combine under a compound query;
- how absence, weakness and shift should be represented;
- what a valid refusal means for retrieval;
- where selective-risk comparisons are non-degenerate;
- whether propagation has attainable headroom over a direct compound model.

C0 is explicitly exploratory. It may branch, discard mechanisms, revise hypotheses and produce unexpected observations. It cannot promote a scientific claim.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

C0 must preserve all of the following:

1. **Option B remains immutable.** No Option B model, language, query, threshold, canonical row, manifest, result or interpretation may be changed to assist Option C.
2. **C0 produces no promoted Option C finding.** Every C0 measurement is exploratory or diagnostic.
3. **C1 calibration and test evidence remain unseen during C0.** No mechanism or threshold may be selected using those rows.
4. **Every explored candidate remains visible.** Failed candidates, discarded variants and negative diagnostics may not be silently removed from the record.
5. **Unexpected findings are preserved but cannot rescue a confirmatory decision.** A new claim requires a new identifier and fresh prospective evidence.
6. **Strong baselines are part of discovery, not an afterthought.** C0 must determine whether the proposed novelty has plausible headroom over them before C1 is authorised.
7. **A C0 failure is claim-scoped.** It does not invalidate B-PREM-001 or prove that no support mechanism could ever work.

## C0 objectives

C0 must answer enough design questions to make a later C1 contract meaningful:

1. What evidence object should represent support for each primitive?
2. Which propagation families behave coherently under the bounded query forms?
3. How do supported, weak, absent and shifted primitives affect acceptance and error?
4. What refusal semantics are meaningful for relation-specific retrieval?
5. Which baseline is strongest at matched coverage?
6. Is there an attainable operating region where a propagation method could materially improve selective risk?
7. Can one candidate mechanism be specified precisely enough for independent recomputation?
8. Is a C1 test justified, or would it merely formalise a mechanism already dominated during development?

C0 may discover that the original Option C framing is wrong. That is an acceptable outcome.

## Five-role data firewall

The Option C data design must use five logically distinct roles:

```text
C0 fit
C0 iteration
C0 selection
C1 calibration reserve
C1 test reserve
```

### C0 fit

Permitted uses:

- fit primitive readouts;
- fit direct compound models;
- fit residual or nonconformity models;
- estimate training-only scaling parameters;
- construct candidate support objects.

### C0 iteration

Permitted uses:

- inspect risk–coverage behaviour;
- diagnose support propagation;
- add or revise candidate mechanisms;
- inspect absent, weak and shifted failure modes;
- tune implementation choices during discovery.

Repeated access is allowed. Every evaluated candidate version must be tied to a repository commit and an append-only registry entry.

### C0 selection

This set remains hidden from candidate-level iteration until the candidate registry closes.

Permitted uses:

- compare the final registered candidate set;
- perform baseline fidelity review;
- select one mechanism for a possible C1 contract;
- decide whether C1 is justified.

C0 selection results are still exploratory. They select a hypothesis; they do not confirm it.

### C1 calibration reserve

This set is used only after the C1 contract merges.

Permitted uses:

- compute the frozen conformal or calibration thresholds declared in C1;
- perform integrity checks explicitly allowed by C1.

It may not be used to select the mechanism, query, nonconformity score, coverage region, baseline, margin or decision rule.

### C1 test reserve

This set is used once for the registered C1 evaluation after calibration completes.

It determines the C1 scientific decision. No post-result rescue experiment is permitted under the same C1 identifier.

## Repository and row separation

Every role must be repository-separated from every other role. A repository may appear in only one Option C role.

C1 reserve repositories must also be disjoint from every repository represented in the canonical Option B selected manifests.

Before C0 mechanism work begins, a data-allocation implementation PR must:

1. reconstruct the frozen eligible source pool under declared parser and tokenizer identities;
2. remove repositories represented in Option B canonical selection;
3. allocate repositories deterministically to the three visible C0 roles and one unselected C1 reserve pool;
4. commit the allocation algorithm, aggregate counts and cryptographic commitments;
5. stop without selecting the final C1 calibration and test rows.

If the remaining CodeSearchNet repository pool is too small or structurally unsuitable, C0 must stop before mechanism evaluation and propose a separately reviewed external-corpus decision. Rows may not be opportunistically borrowed from Option B or another C0 role.

## Deferred C1 selection

Final C1 calibration and test rows must not be selected before the C1 contract is merged.

The C1 contract must freeze a deterministic selection algorithm over the committed reserve pool. The selection seed must be derived from the C1 contract merge commit using a fixed domain-separation string recorded in that contract.

This makes the final row identities unknowable until the mechanism, metric, baselines, material margin and kill rule are already committed.

The selection implementation must refuse:

- an unmerged or ambiguous C1 contract commit;
- a changed reserve-pool commitment;
- repository overlap with Option B or C0;
- reuse of an existing output directory;
- any runtime tuning argument not frozen in C1.

## Bounded discovery space

C0 may investigate a bounded family of support-propagation approaches rather than one preselected mechanism.

Candidate families may include:

1. **coordinate-wise intersection or worst-coordinate support**;
2. **set or interval propagation through the query operator**;
3. **joint residual or nonconformity propagation in predicted primitive space**;
4. **sampling-based propagation of calibrated primitive uncertainty**;
5. **a learned post-hoc support combiner**, provided it is clearly separated from the direct compound baseline and receives no C1 evidence.

This list defines permitted families, not required implementations. C0 may conclude that one or more families are incoherent and omit them with a recorded reason.

C0 may examine at most **three bounded query forms**. Query forms may be revised during C0, but every version must be recorded before its own C0 evaluation. C1 will later freeze at most the subset justified by C0 and the remaining budget.

## Required baselines during C0

C0 must implement or faithfully represent at least:

1. **independent per-primitive split-conformal abstention**;
2. **a directly trained compound model with its own conformal wrapper**;
3. **the candidate propagated-support mechanism or mechanisms**;
4. **an uncalibrated confidence baseline**, where technically meaningful;
5. **an oracle-support diagnostic**, used only to estimate attainable headroom.

The prior-art review must identify the closest applicable post-hoc concept bottleneck, selective prediction, conformal and unseen-query composition methods before any novelty language is written into C1.

If a required baseline cannot be implemented faithfully within C0, C1 is not justified.

## Candidate registry

C0 must maintain an append-only candidate registry.

Every candidate or material revision must record:

- candidate ID and version;
- repository commit SHA;
- support-object definition;
- propagation rule;
- query form;
- nonconformity or confidence score;
- fitting data roles;
- calibration data role used during C0;
- hyperparameters and selection procedure;
- expected failure mode;
- date added;
- status: active, superseded, rejected or selected for C0 selection.

No candidate may be deleted after evaluation. Superseded versions remain visible.

A candidate must be registered before its first evaluation on C0 iteration data. The final candidate set must be closed before C0 selection data are accessed.

## Discovery ledger

C0 must maintain a separate append-only discovery ledger.

Each material observation must be classified as one of:

```text
PLANNED_MECHANISM_DIAGNOSTIC
EXPLORATORY_OBSERVATION
UNEXPECTED_FINDING
NEW_HYPOTHESIS_REQUIRING_FRESH_DATA
IMPLEMENTATION_OR_DATA_INTEGRITY_FINDING
```

Each row must include:

- discovery ID;
- first-observed timestamp and commit;
- data role on which it was first seen;
- whether it was anticipated;
- concise observation;
- affected candidates or assumptions;
- plausible explanations;
- action taken inside C0;
- whether it may influence the C1 contract;
- fresh-evidence requirement for any future claim.

### Discovery-preservation rule

An unexpected finding may alter C0 exploration and may motivate the later C1 design if it is discovered before the C1 contract freezes.

It may not:

- be described as a confirmed finding;
- justify inspecting C1 reserves;
- change the frozen C1 decision after C1 begins;
- rescue a failed C1 result;
- inherit support from B-PREM-001.

A claim arising from an unexpected C1 test observation requires a new experiment identifier and fresh data not used to discover it.

## Required C0 diagnostics

C0 must report development-only diagnostics sufficient to judge discriminating power:

- risk–coverage curves;
- selective risk at multiple visible coverage anchors;
- calibration error or empirical coverage diagnostics appropriate to each method;
- accepted-query counts and error counts;
- refusal reasons by primitive and query form;
- supported, weak, absent and shifted primitive regimes;
- repository-level dispersion;
- attainable oracle-support headroom;
- direct-compound headroom;
- sensitivity to support thresholds or candidate versions;
- failure examples and degenerate operating regions;
- runtime and memory sufficient to assess C1 feasibility.

No single C0 metric is a scientific gate. The complete diagnostic record supports the decision to freeze or abandon C1.

## C0 outputs

C0 must publish a documentation and artifact checkpoint containing:

1. data-allocation identities and firewall verification;
2. the complete candidate registry;
3. the complete discovery ledger;
4. all C0 iteration and C0 selection result identities;
5. baseline implementation and fidelity review;
6. discriminating-power and attainable-headroom analysis;
7. selected mechanism, if any;
8. rejected mechanisms and reasons;
9. proposed C1 query scope;
10. proposed C1 metric and coverage design;
11. explicit C0 outcome;
12. exact remaining working-day budget.

C0 result artifacts must be labelled exploratory and may not create a promoted claim-ledger row.

## C0 outcomes

Exactly one outcome must be recorded:

### `C1_CONTRACT_JUSTIFIED`

Use only when:

- the data firewall is intact;
- all required baselines are faithfully represented;
- the final candidate registry was evaluated on C0 selection data;
- at least one candidate is not uniformly dominated by the strongest baseline across the relevant development operating region;
- non-degenerate coverage and error counts exist;
- attainable headroom exists;
- the selected mechanism can be specified deterministically;
- a meaningful C1 failure remains plausible;
- enough budget remains to implement, independently verify and run C1.

This outcome authorises only a documentation-only C1 contract PR.

### `C1_NOT_JUSTIFIED`

Use when no candidate merits a sealed confirmatory test within the current setting and budget.

This closes the current RELATE propagated-refusal novelty route. It does not invalidate Option B and does not prove that every possible support mechanism is impossible.

### `C0_DATA_FIREWALL_FAILED`

Use when repository separation, reserve integrity or deferred selection cannot be guaranteed.

No C1 experiment may proceed from contaminated evidence.

### `C0_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`

Use when the five-day C0 limit is reached without a defensible C1 design.

No rescue extension is automatic.

## C1 handoff requirements

If C0 records `C1_CONTRACT_JUSTIFIED`, the next PR must freeze all of the following before implementation:

- selected candidate mechanism and exact implementation identity;
- exact query or bounded query set;
- exact supported, weak, absent and shifted regimes;
- exact model-fitting roles;
- exact C1 calibration method;
- exact direct-compound and independent-primitive baselines;
- exact selective-risk definition;
- exact coverage definition and comparison region or anchors;
- exact matched-coverage interpolation or tie procedure;
- exact material margin;
- exact refusal, tie and empty-support handling;
- exact reserve-pool selection algorithm and seed derivation;
- exact C1 outcomes and claim-scoped kill rule;
- deterministic replay requirements;
- independent recomputation boundary;
- final publication artifacts.

No C1 implementation begins in the C0 checkpoint PR.

## Claim-scoped stop rule

C0 and C1 decisions must state precisely what they close.

A C0 or C1 failure may close:

> The current RELATE claim that the selected propagated-support approach materially improves selective risk over the strongest registered calibrated baseline in the bounded Option C setting.

It does not erase:

- B-PREM-001;
- the Option B canonical evidence;
- the E01 methodological findings;
- exploratory observations clearly labelled as such;
- a future unrelated research programme using a new decision, identifier and fresh evidence.

The current programme receives no automatic post-failure rescue experiment.

## Prohibited actions

During C0:

- selecting or inspecting final C1 calibration or test rows;
- using Option B test performance to tune Option C;
- promoting C0 measurements as scientific findings;
- silently removing failed candidates;
- changing the candidate registry after opening C0 selection results;
- omitting the direct compound or independent primitive baseline because it is strong;
- defining coverage so narrowly that a baseline cannot be matched;
- treating more refusal as improvement without matched-coverage risk;
- extending the budget without a new decision;
- writing a C1 pass threshold after observing C1 evidence.

## Methodological additions

This protocol adds three evidence-process rules.

### Discovery-preservation rule

Confirmatory discipline must not erase unexpected observations. Exploratory discoveries are recorded separately and require fresh prospective evidence before promotion.

### Phase-label rule

Every metric and artifact must identify whether it belongs to C0 fit, C0 iteration, C0 selection, C1 calibration or C1 test. A result may not migrate between phases through prose.

### Deferred-holdout rule

When public data make a conventional hidden test set weak, final holdout identities should be derived only after the confirmatory contract freezes, from a previously committed reserve pool and deterministic post-contract seed.
